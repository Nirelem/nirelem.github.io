I’m on the other side of red brick already, ETA about five minutes away from you. Even when you tell me to buy anything from Gonz, you know I’ll just take one order of four gigantic siomai with too much soy sauce on the side. We both insist. Dongyan says ‘hi’ and ‘bye’ as we head back up. I wanted to try the elevator that just got fixed but you tell me to take my time because third year changes you. I think I get what you meant now. And yet I still want to be like you on the first day of RecWeek at Colayco Pavillion. So yes, I will walk you back to U.D to get your guitar amp before WriterSpeac even if it means Derf’s will be just a bit farther. Squeeze my hand as tight as need be, because I know that I’ll never see you again until the next time.

**<u>AUTHOR’S STATEMENT:</u>**
My seniors used to tell me the horrors of heading into what may or may not be the second half of your life into Ateneo — your circle, workload, and life all change at the same time. I used to doubt them, but now that I find myself in the places where they were when they met me (be it Colayco Pavillion, MVP 221, or the spot beside Faura Hall), I can’t help but just… get them. And who they were back then. But above all else, I remember them. I miss them. ‘Graduation Speech’ is not what I would say at the podium should I get the honor of delivering one when I am a senior, but it’s just a list of jumbled things and timelines that I shared with people whose ETAs are farther than five minutes.
