a renewed visa card for a country you’ll never visit again;  
candid shots of your friend sleeping in class;  
phone-stolen selfies, taken from purposefully bad angles.  
  
fanart of your favorite ship, saved from twitter;  
a hat, a stage, a bouquet of flowers — pictures with  
too few of your favorite people.  
  
shoddy face paint, poses in costume;  
lit candles for a friend’s debut —  
you haven’t spoken since high school.  
  
your oldest friend, ears pricked and snout refusing the camera;  
your best friends, sitting in a mall food court for the last time;  
your farewell gift, plastered with faces and notes from people you love.  
  
a selfie at the gates of your newest adventure;  
an ID picture, an index card; mismatched schedules  
and no chances to properly meet up.  
  
three stray kittens, exploring their tiny world;  
a sign-up sheet, two QR codes; cheap canteen lunch,  
eaten with your first new friend!  
  
powerpoint slides, shakily taken with blurry text;  
“this is so us,”from pinterest, sent through discord dms;  
art pieces that you’re proud of, writing that you aren’t;  
  
proof-of-life selfies sent all the way back home.  
leaves torn out of the garden and pressed  
into your microscope, flattened for your viewing pleasure;  
  
candids of your blockmates — you were never close,  
but they look happy in these shots;  
fire emojis on a stupid picture — an inside joke with friends.  
  
the schedule for an oral exam, pinned on  
cheap corkboard and littered with a hundred fingermarks;  
a final picture with the class — prof, come and join!  
  
a goodbye dinner: see you next semester.  
the clear blue sky, framed by beige plane windows;  
and finally that bright red gate, welcoming you home.  
  
**<u>AUTHOR’S STATEMENT:</u>**
Though not even close to epic or heroic, I hope Calliope blesses my work nonetheless. This is a
more personal piece, as it quite literally draws from moments in my own life, and the nostalgia I feel
for the subtler things. I wanted to reminisce on the more mundane moments in life, snapshots of
things both big and little that you don’t really think about when you recall them in your mind. I
think that the transience of these little moments, immortalized in digital form, aligns well with the
theme of transition in reference, and to an overall theme of living with the moments life throws at
you, and using them as motivation for the future.
