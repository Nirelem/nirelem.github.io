<div class="poem-body">

<p style="margin:0;">someone lit a fire onto dried palm leaves and wood gathered on the roadside where the grass browned from the noon's overbearing gaze. there were pallbearers ascending a hill, carrying a white casket with silver handles and silver lining. and i watched them carry my uncle's body to the peak of the hill to be laid with other bodies of uncles and aunts, of fathers and mothers and children, like fish piled to be counted after the catch.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0;">my mother would join the fish soon. (followed by my father, then me and my sister, like salmon following the memory of their dead mother's scent to return upstream to live and die.) she already booked a service with St. Peter Life Plan in case our bathroom mold decided to grow and suck the moisture from her body and turn into tough scales that would suffocate her.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0;">if i could, i'd die alone.</p>
<div class="poem-split"><span>my mother said</span><span><em>but that is too scary.</em></span></div>
<p style="margin:0;">so is resting in a hill of bodies, regardless of relation or strangeness, where we become a collective pile of cheesy pus that salts the earth; where we become fossils for future archeologists to find and misidentify. they'd call me a <em>Homo sapien</em> from the 21st century,</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 6em;">female from the number of ribs and the pelvis shape,</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 6em;">adolescent from the number of teeth.</p>
<p style="margin:0;">but they won't discuss who this <em>Homo sapien</em> was in the 21st century, nor will they know that many often mistook this female for a male from her voice, nor that this adolescent used to compare her mottledness and flabbiness to the bones of her peers.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<div class="poem-split"><span>my sister asked</span><span><em>how do you wanna die?</em></span></div>
<div class="poem-split"><span>i said</span><span><em>return me to the water from where life came.</em></span></div>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 2em;">i want to be a whale. not because i'm fat, but because their bodies</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 18em;">sink to the seabed and</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 15em;">become food for others. i want fish</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 16em;">to eat my carrion and bacteria.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 2em;">if not a whale, a coral. we are both calcium. rearrange my bones so that i'll be a calcified bush, and you won't recognize me once the algae stick.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0 0 0 15em;">from there, we become oxygen.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 10em;">saltwater will weather my bones to grow pores, and</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 10em;">they'll be more beautiful than acne scars.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0; text-align:right;"><em>what about returning to dust?</em></p>
<p style="margin:0;">sure, i don't mind.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0 0 0 2em;">but don't place me in an ornate jar to remain stagnant for ogling.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 2em;">sprinkle me on the shoreline of a painting. i'll be the rocks, shells, and seaweed. i'll touch the sea foam like Adam touched fingers with God. critics always talk about how God's drapery takes the form of the brain; rather than God or Adam, i'll be the sky and the hill, and even the shore and the riverbed hidden out of frame. i'll be a better conversation starter as a painting than a jar.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0; text-align:right;"><em>and where do you think you'll go after death?</em></p>
<p style="margin:0;">sea glass. <em>how?</em> we're bottles drifting at sea from the moment we're thrown into it to deliver our Mother's message. we tumble and sink and rise and break, until we arrive on the shore polished. when light hits us, we become starlight.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="margin:0;">so, don't bury me. make me a whale.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 6em;">coral.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 6em;">painting.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 0 6em;">starlight.</p>

</div>

**<u>AUTHOR'S STATEMENT:</u>**
"burial will" is the fifth poem in a suite of five poems that I wrote for Poetry Workshop 2. The suite
is kind of like my journey to accepting my body, so it has themes of body dysmorphia, using disgust
and obsession/yearning as its core emotions, nature as biological imagery, and some religious
themes as part of the voice. I am very big on prosody, so my poems are meant to be read out loud. I
was inspired a lot by Ruel de Vera's "Gambaphobia," Céline Sciamma's *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*,
Flower Face's "Spiracle," and many of Sleep Token's songs.

Unlike "note on a self-portrait," "burial will" is the climax of the suite, where the persona's mindset
shifts into a desire to be more than the body—to be one with nature and to be natural and
important—rather than just an exploration of it. It was heavily inspired by a conversation I had
with my sister about how each of us wanted to die, and I told her that there were organizations that
can use your bones (or crematorial remains) to revive coral reefs, which I still think is a better use of
my body than simply burying it. In regards to feedback, I am open to any kind of constructive
criticism and other comments!
